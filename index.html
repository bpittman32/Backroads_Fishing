How to Play

1

Cast

Hold CAST. The power meter swings up and back down, so let go at the top for a long cast. Short casts reach shallow fish near the dock; the deep water is far out.

2

Tempt

Hold REEL to drag the lure in, tap TWITCH to make it dart, or do nothing and let it sink. Each species prefers one of the three. Watch a fish turn toward your lure.

3

Hook

The fish nibbles a few times first. Stay still. When the lure plunges and a ! appears, tap the red HOOK! button in the middle fast. Too early spooks it. Too late and it steals your worm or swallows the hook, which makes it worth far less. A fish that gets away won't bite again. TWITCH never sets the hook, so twitch freely to attract fish.

4

Fight

Once hooked, the bottom of the screen becomes one big fight pad. Hold it to reel, lift your thumb to give line. The fish takes turns resting (green marker) and running (red marker). Reel hard while it rests. When the marker flashes, it's about to run: let go before the tension bar hits the red or the line snaps. Small fish can be reeled straight through a run; big ones can't. Bass, trout, pickerel and bowfin sometimes race to the surface and jump (blue marker). Keep reeling through a jump: slack line lets them throw the hook. Drag: the − and + above the reel pad set how hard the fish can pull before line slips off the reel. Loose drag stops snapped lines and lets you fight fish heavier than your line, but you can't gain line while it slips, cranking against it twists the line until it breaks, and a fish that runs far enough takes all your line. Tight drag works like plain line.

Reading the pond

  • Before you cast, drag across the water to look out over the pond. The view stays put after you let go, then drifts back to the dock after a few seconds.
  • Every species lives in the pond around the clock. Outside its feeding times it rests (perch settle on the bottom at night, catfish lie low by day, eels bury themselves in the mud) and mostly ignores your bait. The Fish Log lists when each one feeds.
  • Tap the clock at the top to rest until another time of day or travel to new water. Time runs while you fish. Dawn, day, dusk and night each bring different species to the bite.
  • Weather shifts on its own. Rain stirs up catfish, trout and bass; fog brings out shy fish but makes the water hard to see.
  • Perch, bluegill, crappie and carp travel in groups. Hook one and its schoolmates crowd your lure, and after you land it the rest are still nearby.
  • Catfish and eels hunt by smell, so darkness doesn't stop them finding a worm. Pickerel hide in the weeds and dart out at fast lures. Carp spook easily, so cast past them.
  • Crappie have paper-thin mouths: keep the tension light or the hook tears out. Carp make long runs, and eels spin.
  • Each fish has a depth. Floating lures stay on top, the jig drops to the bottom.
  • At level 10 you can rent a boat on Big Rock Lake (40 coins a trip) for walleye, pike and tiger muskie. At level 15, buy chest waders for Iron Ferry River and its catfish, walleye and drum.
  • Reach angler level 5 and buy a Trout Stamp to fish Willow Creek (tap the clock while fishing). The current carries bait downstream toward you; free-line, split-shot and bobber rigs drift naturally and fish love it. Fish hold behind boulders facing upstream. Heavy sinkers and deep lures snag on rocks.
  • Rods come as complete setups with their own line, and each remembers its own tackle. The ultralight casts tiny baits far and keeps crappie hooked but breaks on big fish. The baitcaster throws frogs and jigs and muscles fish out of the weeds but tears panfish mouths. The catfish rod launches heavy bottom rigs farthest. Tackle outside a rod's range casts short. Each rod can be upgraded twice.
  • Big fish can thrash free in the last few feet unless you have a landing net.
  • Tap your angler on the dock to see your level, stats and Fish Log, and to switch your Fish Finder, shades and lantern on or off. Your angler level rises as you cast and catch. Each level casts farther (up to twice the starting distance) and sets hooks more reliably. Beginners' casts land short or long of where they aimed.
  • Far water holds bigger, rarer fish that spook less. The view follows your lure out there.
  • Bait (worms, crickets, minnows, chicken liver, dough balls and corn) all use your hook and weight rig. Liver draws catfish, bullheads and eels by smell, dough and corn are carp baits, minnows keep swimming for predators, and crickets kick for sunfish. Each fish you land or lose uses one bait.
  • Bait goes on a rig, built in the Tackle Box: Free-line, Split-shot, Bobber, Carolina, Drop-shot or Three-way. A bobber holds bait at the depth you set and dips on nibbles, then goes under on the bite. In spring, bass and sunfish guard round, pale beds in the shallows. Land a lure right on a bed and the guard hits it out of anger, and releasing a guard earns extra luck. Panfish sometimes lift the bait instead and the bobber lies flat: that's a bite too. A fixed bobber shows nibbles best but casts short when set deep; a slip bobber casts far at any depth and stands up once the bait has sunk into place. A bobber lying on its side after it settles means the bait is on the bottom. Carolina and three-way rigs keep bait near the bottom, and a drop-shot holds it a leader's length above it. Match the hook to the fish's mouth (the Fish Log lists it).
  • Small-mouthed fish tend to swallow a worm if you're slow. Lures almost never get swallowed.
  • Lures come in natural, bright and dark colors. Natural works in clear daylight, bright in rain and fog, dark at dusk and night. The Tackle Box marks the best one.
  • Small lures draw panfish. Large lures draw big fish and carry bigger hooks.
  • Wary fish see thick line in clear water. Light line works better on them, if you can keep it from breaking.
  • Releasing a fish pays less but raises your luck with rare fish.
  • Every species has bronze, silver and gold trophy lengths, listed in the Fish Log. Trophies earn bonus XP.
  • Lost the line? You lose the lure with it. Worms are free forever.

Help improve the game

Share play stats?

Backroads Fishing can send a little anonymous information to its developer. It helps find bugs and shows which spots and devices people play on.

  • Error reports when something breaks: the version, the error message, and the kind of device.
  • A short summary each time you put the game away: minutes played, which spots, how many fish, your level, the kind of device and screen size.
  • Your angler card: angler name, level, respect, species caught and spots fished.

Nothing else from your phone: no contacts, no location, no other apps. You're identified only by a random ID the game made up, plus the angler name you already show on the leaderboard.
